Kyoto: Fushimi Inari to Nishiki

Tue, Oct 27 · Kyoto day 1, south and east Kyoto
Est. ~16,000 steps (~11.5 km), or ~19,000 with the Philosopher's Path
7:30
Osaka to Fushimi InaritravelJR Special Rapid to Kyoto (about 30 minutes), then the JR Nara Line to Inari Station (5 minutes).
8:30-10:30
Fushimi Inari TaishaThousands of red torii gates winding up a forested hill. Go early to avoid crowds. The lower part is easy, and the full climb has many stairs, so go as far as you like and turn back.See photos
~11:15
Kiyomizu-deraA big wooden temple on a hillside with a large terrace and city views. The approach is uphill and busy, so take your time.See photos
12:45
Sannenzaka & Ninenzaka, lunchfoodStone-paved lanes of old shops and teahouses on the way down from Kiyomizu-dera. Walking here is part of the fun. Good place for lunch, sweets and tea.See photos
~14:00
Gion & Hanamikoji StreetOld wooden teahouses. Geiko and maiko are most often seen around dusk, so you may or may not spot one at this time.See photos
~15:30
Nishiki Market, short visitfoodA narrow covered market, known as Kyoto's kitchen. Keep it to about 30-40 minutes: walk the lane once, snack on skewers or tofu, and look at the knife shops (Aritsugu is here, if you want your knife). Many stalls close around 17:00, so arrive by 15:30.See photos
~17:00
Back to OsakatravelHankyu Line from Kawaramachi to Umeda is about 45 minutes. Dinner near the hotel.
Optional
Philosopher's Path & Nanzen-jinice to haveA calm canal path of about 2 km lined with trees, running between Nanzen-ji temple and Ginkaku-ji. It's quiet and scenic, but it's in the north-east, away from Nishiki. It does not fit with Nishiki the same afternoon, so swap it in for Gion and Nishiki if you prefer quiet to markets. Needs about 2 hours.See photos
Long day. If tired, skip Gion and go from the Sannenzaka lanes straight to Nishiki Market.

Est. ~17,000 steps (~12 km)

Osaka Water Bus (Aqualiner)book ticketAn hour-long boat loop through the city's rivers and canals, passing under bridges with views of the castle. Check the schedule, since service changes by season.See photos

Osaka CastleA large castle with moats, stone walls and a big park. Easy walking around the park. The castle interior has many steps, so it's optional.See photos
~12:45
Lunch near the castlefoodPark food stalls, or a short walk toward the next stop.
~13:30
Osaka Museum of HistoryA museum beside the castle park, with life-size recreations of old Osaka streets. From its upper floors you get a view of the castle and the ruins of the old Naniwa Palace. Check opening hours and closed days.See photos
~15:00
Shitenno-ji TempleOne of Japan's oldest Buddhist temples, founded in the 6th century, with a five-story pagoda and a flat layout. A short subway ride south of the castle on the Tanimachi Line.See photos
~16:15
Osaka Tenmangu & Tenjinbashisuji shopping streetbrowseA shrine, then one of Japan's longest covered shopping arcades with local snacks, small shops and eateries. Walk as much as you like, since it's flat and fun.See photos
~17:45-19:45
Onsen with an outdoor bathcheck firstAbout 2 hours in a tattoo-friendly public onsen with an open-air bath, in the Kita-ku / Umeda area (candidate: Tennen Onsen Naniwa no Yu; Radium Onsen and Healthy Spa Tateba are alternatives). It is a public bath (men's side), so you can go in together and meet at the exit. Verify the address, opening hours and tattoo rule before going. Bring a small towel, or rent one there.

Dinner near the hotelfood
